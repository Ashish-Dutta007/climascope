## Findings to challenge

1. **The same side maps appear in every focused scene.** The centre shows three periods of the primary metric. Each side shows one other metric for 2050–2079, plus its three regional means. Month, region and selected member are consistent. These are absolute maps, not change or uncertainty maps.
2. **Their visual weight is too high for supporting material.** In open mode, the centre is 5,053 px wide; each side is 3,158 px. The supporting panels together occupy 56% of the panel width. Wider panels do not make the height-limited Scotland maps taller.
3. **Relevance varies by scene.** Precipitation and temperature give water-balance context, but do not by themselves explain its calculation. The same side maps contribute little to the land-cover or member-spread comparison. The land-cover headline also repeats the all-cell threshold share; it is not a land-cover-specific result.
4. **The original wording repeated itself.** A scene title, a long question, a numerical caption and a method paragraph competed for attention. Side panels included generic reading advice. A single side map's legend said “All three maps”.
5. **Direction ties were one-sided.** Angus precipitation in July has six members increasing and six decreasing in 2050–2079. The old headline reported only the six increases. The candidate shows `6 : 6`, labelled `increase : decrease`. Check that this reads clearly.

## Options worth comparing

- **Focused climate comparison:** three absolute period maps plus two change maps (each future minus baseline) could make all five maps useful. Change maps need their own clearly labelled, zero-centred scale fixed across months, checked missing-cell handling, and data support. Do not reuse the absolute legend. Retain other-metric context as an option if it proves useful.
- **Water balance context:** assess whether PT evapotranspiration would be more informative than Tmax. Check the actual CWB/PT definition and matching source columns before presenting a water-budget explanation.
- **Land cover:** consider an actual mapped land-cover panel and give the within-class comparison more room. Confirm dataset/year and fractional-cover interpretation first. Do not present overlap as projected habitat loss.
- **Member spread:** give the member plots more prominence. A spatial spread/agreement map would require cell-level member calculations; the current regional-mean summaries cannot supply it.
