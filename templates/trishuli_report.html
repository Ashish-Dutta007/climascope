Bhote Koshi–Trishuli flood

A geospatial reading of the mass-movement record, the terrain and the public river gauges from 26 August.

Ashish Dutta · Geospatial researcher · 27 August 2026

Not emergency guidance. This is an independent reconstruction, not an official warning or damage assessment. Follow Nepal’s authorities and local responders for safety information.

At 08:37:10 NPT, USGS recorded a large landslide high above the border. The mapped point sits at about 5,590 m, with 3,838 m of vertical difference to the Rasuwagadhi gauge. That places a major mass movement at the right time and in a very energetic landscape. It does not, on its own, explain where all the water came from.

The first post-event image, and a wider channel

Optical cover arrived on 27 August. Downstream of the gorge it is clear enough to measure.

Scene cloud cover for 27 August is 78 per cent, but that is an average over a 110 km tile. Read along the channel itself, 71 per cent of the river is usable, and the clear part is the lower corridor through Betrawati, Bidur and Devighat. The gorge above stays covered.

Comparing only pixels free of cloud on both dates, within 600 m of the mapped channel, the wetted area grows from 0.93 to 1.72 km², an increase of 85 per cent. Loosening or tightening the water threshold moves that figure between 72 and 133 per cent but does not change its direction. Across 46 cross-sections clear on both dates, median wetted width goes from 60 to 120 m, and 40 of the 46 are wider.

This measures a difference between two dates. It does not attribute one. Both fall inside the monsoon, so part of the change is ordinary discharge variation, and at 20 m pixels a 60 m channel is three pixels across. It is an observation awaiting the radar pass, not a flood extent.

What is still open

Three things the current evidence does not settle.

Rock-and-ice flow

The USGS solution gives a location, not a volume, and no deposit has been traced from scar to channel. Needs stereo or sub-metre optical.

Debris dam and breach

DHM’s preliminary reading. An impoundment leaves slack water upstream, which radar detects through cloud. The 28 August pass covers this.

An additional water store

Kept open by the reported water volume. Testing needs before and after lake shorelines; recent Sentinel-2 scenes were 51, 78 and 97 per cent cloud.

Sources and limits

Everything used above is listed here, including what this work cannot establish.

Primary evidence
  • USGS reviewed event — M 5.2 landslide, origin time and source location.
  • Nepal DHM station observations — public point readings and station warning thresholds.
  • DHM preliminary interpretation — blockage distance and proposed temporary-lake sequence, reported by The Kathmandu Post.
  • ICIMOD advisory — observed river behaviour and cause uncertainty.
  • Copernicus DEM and Data Space — endpoint terrain measurements and acquisition catalogue.
What is not claimed
  • The USGS point is not a mapped collapse polygon. Endpoint relief is not avalanche runout or a hydraulic model.
  • Gauge silence is not proof of instrument destruction and is not used to calculate flood-wave speed.
  • The preliminary blockage account is attributed, not independently verified here.
  • No flood extent, depth, discharge, affected-asset count, casualty figure or causal climate attribution is produced.
