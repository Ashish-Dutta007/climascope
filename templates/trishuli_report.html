The clearest result so far is downstream. In the first usable post-event Sentinel-2 image, median wetted width across 46 cloud-free cross-sections changes from 60 to 120 m; 40 of the 46 are wider. It is a measured difference between 12 and 27 August, not a flood footprint and not an explanation of the trigger.

A coarse rainfall screen does not explain the source surge

The high-altitude source grid was nearly dry; the Nepal-side headwaters had ordinary monsoon rain.

This is a rapid model-grid screen, not a mountain rain-gauge network. It makes intense source-area rainfall a poor fit to the available record, but cannot exclude local precipitation missed by a coarse grid.

USGS has revised the source record

The initial M 4.4 earthquake report is now an M 5.2 glacial collapse and debris flow.

In key points updated on 26 August at 18:44 UTC, USGS says nearby seismic stations, long-period waves and satellite imagery led it to determine that no earthquake occurred. The source location was estimated from satellite images. This materially strengthens the source attribution; it does not by itself resolve temporary damming or the origin of the full water volume.

Then four returned gauge series go quiet

Dhunche continues beyond the chart window; the four other public series do not.

See the event-window station readings

A dashboard may still show these stations as “below warning level”. That label belongs to the final sample received; it says nothing about the unobserved peak or present safety.

ICIMOD separately reports a rise of as much as 9 m in 30 minutes at Galchhi, citing hydrological observations. That value is attributed here because it is not present in the public DHM series retrieved for this audit.

The first post-event image, and a wider channel

Optical cover arrived on 27 August. Downstream of the gorge it is clear enough to measure.

Scene cloud cover for 27 August is 78 per cent, but that is an average over a 110 km tile. Read along the channel itself, 71 per cent of the river is usable, and the clear part is the lower corridor through Betrawati, Bidur and Devighat. The gorge above stays covered.

Comparing only pixels free of cloud on both dates, within 600 m of the mapped channel, the wetted area grows from 0.93 to 1.72 km², an increase of 85 per cent. Loosening or tightening the water threshold moves that figure between 72 and 133 per cent but does not change its direction. Across 46 cross-sections clear on both dates, median wetted width goes from 60 to 120 m, and 40 of the 46 are wider.

The new Planet Crisis Response collection includes 27 August SkySat imagery at about 0.8 m. It is sharper, but opaque cloud covers Rasuwagadhi and valley haze covers the channel at Syabrubesi. I have not used it for quantitative change mapping; higher resolution is not better evidence when the target is obscured.

This measures a difference between two dates. It does not attribute one. Both fall inside the monsoon, so part of the change is ordinary discharge variation, and at 20 m pixels a 60 m channel is three pixels across. It is an observation awaiting the radar pass, not a flood extent.

What remains unresolved

ICIMOD suspects an ice-rock avalanche, while saying the cause remains unconfirmed.

Exact source geometry and volume

USGS now determines a glacial collapse and debris flow, but no scar-to-channel deposit or volume has been mapped in this note. Testing needs clear stereo or sub-metre optical imagery.

Debris dam and breach

DHM and ICIMOD are assessing a temporary blockage. An impoundment may leave slack water or deposits upstream; the 28 August radar pass can test for those signatures through cloud.

An additional water store

The present imagery does not establish drainage from a lake or another stored-water source. Testing needs before-and-after shorelines; the relevant upper catchment remains cloud-covered.

A supraglacial-lake drainage and roughly 20 million m³ explanation circulating for Rasuwa refers to the July 2025 flood. ICIMOD’s 26 August 2026 advisory does not make that attribution for this event.

Sources and limits

Everything used above is listed here, including what this work cannot establish.

Primary evidence
  • USGS reviewed event — M 5.2 landslide, origin time and source location.
  • Nepal DHM station observations — public point readings and station warning thresholds.
  • DHM preliminary interpretation — blockage distance and proposed temporary-lake sequence, reported by The Kathmandu Post.
  • ICIMOD advisory — observed river behaviour and cause uncertainty.
  • Copernicus DEM and Data Space — endpoint terrain measurements and Sentinel-2 acquisition catalogue.
  • Open-Meteo — rapid 14-point model-grid rainfall screening.
  • Planet Crisis Response — visual review of post-event PlanetScope and SkySat coverage; not used quantitatively.
What is not claimed
  • The USGS point is not a mapped collapse polygon. Endpoint relief is not avalanche runout or a hydraulic model.
  • Gauge silence is not proof of instrument destruction and is not used to calculate flood-wave speed.
  • The preliminary blockage account is attributed, not independently verified here.
  • No flood extent, depth, discharge, affected-asset count, casualty figure or causal climate attribution is produced.
