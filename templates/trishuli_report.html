01

Executive summary

A large, rainfall-independent surge entered Nepal from Tibet down the Lhende/Bhote Koshi on the morning of 26 August 2026 and propagated into the Trishuli. The meteorological evidence is strong and points away from rainfall; the satellite evidence does not yet exist.

Rainfall is effectively excluded at source High confidence

Open-Meteo reanalysis over the Tibetan source catchment gives 0.5–0.8 mm of precipitation in the 72 hours before onset, and 0.1–0.3 mm in the final 24 hours. This independently corroborates the Rasuwa Chief District Officer's statement that there had been no rain. A rain-generated flash flood of this magnitude is not consistent with these totals.

A cryospheric or mass-movement source is the leading hypothesis Plausible, unverified

ICIMOD attributes the flood to an avalanche in the Lhende Khola. Modelled freezing level over the source catchment sat at 5,480–6,280 m through the preceding 72 hours, placing melt conditions across nearly the whole glacierised band. That is a permissive condition, not proof.

The seismic trigger is genuinely ambiguous Unresolved

USGS and EMSC both catalogue an mb 4.4 at 02:52:47 UTC (08:37:47 NPT), about 34 km from Rasuwagadhi. But depth is fixed at 10 km in both solutions, no moment tensor was published, and the origin time falls after the reported ~08:30 flood arrival. Whether this is a trigger, an aftershock, or the seismic signature of the mass movement itself cannot be decided from catalogue metadata.

Terrain analysis independently flags the worst-hit reach Observed

A blind slope and confinement analysis of the Copernicus 30 m DEM ranks river chainage 16.1 km (28.1545 N, 85.3289 E, at Syabrubesi) as the single most blockage-susceptible reach: 49% of the near-channel area steeper than 45°, 738 m of relief within 500 m, channel gradient 54 m/km. This was computed from terrain alone and matches where the heaviest damage is reported.

03

Preliminary findings

Four independent lines of evidence, in descending order of how firmly the data supports them.

3.1  Precipitation: the source catchment was dry

The contrast is the finding. Across the Tibetan source catchment, including the epicentral area at 6,314 m, 72-hour totals sit between 0.5 and 0.8 mm. Nepal-side corridor points recorded ordinary monsoon rain, 17–60 mm over the same window, which is unremarkable for late August and not close to flash-flood generating in catchments this size. The wettest corridor point over the previous 7 days was Devighat at 145 mm.

Caveat that matters: this is model reanalysis on a coarse grid, not rain gauges. High-altitude Tibetan precipitation is among the least well constrained fields in any global model, and the grid snapped Betrawati and Bidur into a single cell. The finding is robust to that uncertainty only because the deficit is an order of magnitude, not a marginal difference.

3.2  Terrain: where the corridor is primed to block

Mean slope across the corridor window is 30.2°, with the 90th percentile at 44.5°. This is terrain where valley walls deliver material directly into the channel. The index peaks at chainage 16.1 km (Syabrubesi), 21.2 km, and 27.2 km, all in the confined upper gorge. Below roughly chainage 43 km the valley opens, susceptibility collapses toward zero, and the dominant process changes from debris supply and blockage to inundation and siltation, which is consistent with the reported silting at Trishuli Bazaar, Battar and Devighat.

Satellite: what exists, and when the first usable image lands

The single most important operational fact in this assessment: as of 16:45 UTC on 26 August, no post-event Sentinel scene exists over the corridor. The catalogue was queried directly and returned only Sentinel-5P atmospheric products and CGLS land-surface temperature for today.

Use orbit 85 ascending, 28 Aug 12:21 UTC Priority

This is the first post-event acquisition and it has a geometrically matched pre-event twin on 16 Aug at 12:21 UTC, same track, same look angle, 12-day baseline. That pairing is what makes a clean change detection possible. Expect the product online within roughly 3 to 6 hours of acquisition.

Then orbit 121 descending, 31 Aug 00:10 UTC Confirmation

Pre-event twin on 19 Aug. A second, independent look geometry three days later, which matters in this terrain because a single SAR geometry in a steep gorge suffers layover and shadow that can be mistaken for water.

Orbit 19 descending, 5 Sep 00:18 UTC Best baseline, latest

Its pre-event twin, 24 Aug 00:18 UTC, is only two days before onset and is therefore the cleanest possible "before" state, but the post-event half is ten days away.

Optical is a poor bet this week Low expectation

Sentinel-2 scene cloud cover over the corridor ran 51%, 78% and 97% on 24, 22 and 17 August. The next R119 pass falls around 27 August. In active monsoon, plan on radar and treat any clear optical scene as a bonus.

07

24 to 72 hour outlook

Model-based and uncertain. This outlook rests on numerical weather prediction and terrain screening, not on observation of the current channel state. No post-event imagery informs it. It should be superseded by the 28 August radar pass and by any ground reporting, both of which outrank it.

Rainfall adds moderate, not extreme, stress Moderate confidence

Open-Meteo forecasts 17–19 mm over the next 72 h at the Rasuwa headwater points and 16–63 mm along the Nepal corridor, peaking at Benighat (63 mm) and Devighat (38 mm). This is normal monsoon loading. It is not forecast to generate a second flood on its own, but it falls on slopes already destabilised and on channels already choked with fresh debris.

Secondary landslide and debris-flow risk is the main concern Moderate confidence

The upper gorge between chainage 4 and 34 km combines slopes above 45°, 500–750 m of local relief, and now a freshly scoured channel margin. Rain on newly exposed, undercut slopes is the classic setting for follow-on failures in the days after a surge. Reaches 16.1, 21.2, 27.2 and 7.1 km rank highest.

Landslide-dam breach is the highest-consequence scenario Unquantified

If any blockage formed in the upper gorge, an impounded reach can fail suddenly and produce a second surge with little warning. Whether any such impoundment exists is unknown and is precisely what the 28 August radar pass can answer. This scenario cannot be assigned a probability from available data.

Downstream wave has largely passed the corridor Model estimate

On the celerity range used here, the initial wave would have reached Narayangadh between roughly 14:20 and 24:00 NPT on 26 August and Tribeni overnight. The residual downstream signal over the next 24 h is more likely elevated stage, heavy sediment and debris rafts than a sharp flood peak. Chitwan and Nawalparasi remain exposed to any second surge from upstream.

Change-detection workflow, to run the moment the 28 Aug scene lands

# Sentinel-1 GRD, VV, log-ratio change detection.
# Pre : S1D_IW_GRDH_1SDV_20260816T122141 ... orbit 85 ASC
# Post: orbit 85 ASC acquisition of 2026-08-28T12:21Z

1. Preprocess both scenes identically
   apply orbit file -> thermal noise removal -> border noise removal
   -> radiometric calibration to sigma0 -> Range-Doppler terrain correction
   using Copernicus DEM 30 m -> convert to dB. Same DEM, same pixel
   spacing (10 m), same output grid for both dates.

2. Speckle filtering
   Refined Lee 5x5, or a multitemporal filter across the pre-event stack.
   Filter AFTER calibration and BEFORE differencing.

3. Difference
   d = sigma0_post_dB - sigma0_pre_dB
   New open water: strong NEGATIVE d (smooth water backscatters away).
   New coarse deposits / debris: POSITIVE d (rough surface).

4. Threshold
   Do not hard-code a threshold. Fit a bimodal histogram over the valley
   floor mask and take the Otsu or Kittler-Illingworth split. Report the
   threshold you used, and the extent's sensitivity to +/- 1 dB on it.

5. Mask the terrain artefacts, which is essential here
   Slopes > 45 deg cover ~30% of this corridor. Compute layover and
   shadow masks from the DEM and the scene's incidence geometry and
   DELETE them from the result. Radar shadow mimics water perfectly.
   Also mask permanent water from the pre-event scene so you report
   only NEW water.

6. Validate before publishing
   Require agreement with the 31 Aug orbit 121 DESC pair before
   treating any patch as real. A feature seen in one geometry only,
   in terrain this steep, is an artefact until proven otherwise.

7. Intersect with exposure
   Only now join to the HOT layers to produce affected-asset counts.
10

Limitations and next actions

Limitations, stated plainly

  • No post-event imagery exists. Every statement about what the flood did on the ground comes from news and official reporting, cited as such, not from analysis performed here.
  • No gauge data. Nepal DHM exposes no public API at the paths tried. Discharge, stage and actual wave timing are all unmeasured here. The travel-time table is a nomogram, not a forecast.
  • Rainfall is modelled, not observed. Open-Meteo best_match on a coarse grid, in the terrain where global models perform worst. The source-catchment finding survives this only because the signal is an order of magnitude.
  • IMERG was not retrieved. It requires an Earthdata Login bearer token. This is the single most valuable missing dataset.
  • The blockage index is a screening heuristic. Three normalised terrain terms with chosen weights (0.40 slope, 0.30 relief, 0.30 gradient). It is not calibrated against any landslide inventory and its absolute values carry no physical meaning. Its rank ordering is the usable part.
  • The DEM predates the event and is a surface model, so it includes canopy and produced a few spurious negative gradients over water.
  • OSM completeness is uneven in rural Rasuwa. Exposure counts are lower bounds, and the HOT AOI stops at Bidur, so the Dhading, Gorkha, Chitwan and Nawalparasi reaches have no exposure analysis here.
  • Causal attribution is not settled and cannot be settled from this data. The earthquake-versus-mass-movement question in particular requires waveform analysis that catalogue metadata cannot substitute for.
